DS101 Data Systems - Module handbook 2026-27

Quillmere Institute is a fictional institution. This material was written for the CourseFlow retrieval benchmark and describes no real course.

Welcome

DS101 introduces the design and use of relational databases. Over twelve weeks you will learn to model a domain, turn the model into a schema that protects its own integrity, query it with SQL, and understand enough about indexes and transactions to explain why a database behaves as it does under load. The module is a prerequisite for DS201 Distributed Data and for the final-year data engineering project.

This handbook is the authoritative source for how the module is run: teaching, assessment, submission, extensions, academic integrity and support. Each coursework brief describes its own task and deadline; where a brief and this handbook appear to disagree about a rule that applies to all coursework, the handbook applies and you should tell the module leader so that the brief can be corrected.

Teaching pattern

Each week there is one two-hour lecture and one two-hour lab. Lectures introduce concepts and are recorded; recordings appear on the module page within two working days. Labs are not recorded. They are where you practise, ask questions and get feedback, and several of them have sign-offs that you must complete in your own timetabled session.

The module page lists the reading for each week. The core text is referred to in lectures as the module text; the library holds reference copies, and the electronic edition can be read online from the library catalogue.

Submitting coursework

All coursework is submitted electronically through the coursework portal, which records the time of submission. You may resubmit as often as you like before the deadline; only the last submission is marked. Check the confirmation email after every submission, and keep it until marks are released.

Attach a completed cover sheet to every submission. The cover sheet asks you to confirm that the work is your own and to declare any tools you used that the brief or this handbook requires you to declare.

Late submission

Work submitted after the deadline without an approved extension loses ten percentage points for each calendar day it is late, for up to five days; after that it receives a mark of zero. A submission that is one minute late counts as one day late. The penalty is applied to the mark the work would otherwise have received, and it cannot take the mark below zero.

The portal occasionally becomes slow in the last hour before a popular deadline. Technical problems on your own computer or network are not grounds for removing a late penalty, so aim to submit well before the deadline. If the portal itself fails, the module leader will announce an adjusted deadline on the module page.

Extensions

If illness or another serious circumstance outside your control stops you from submitting on time, you can ask for an extension of up to ten working days. To request an extension, submit form EC-7 to the Student Hub no later than the published deadline. The form asks for a short description of the circumstances and, where possible, supporting evidence. Requests made after the deadline are considered only if the circumstances also prevented you from making the request in time.

The Student Hub replies within three working days. Do not stop working on the coursework while you wait: if the request is refused, the original deadline still applies. Extensions apply to coursework only; for the examination, use the separate process for deferral described on the Student Hub pages.

Academic integrity

Everything you submit must be your own work unless the brief explicitly allows group work. Copying another student's work, allowing your work to be copied, working jointly on an individual assessment, and submitting text or code from any source without acknowledgement are all academic misconduct. Suspected cases are referred to the academic integrity panel, whose penalties range from a mark of zero for the component to failure of the module.

Discussing ideas with other students is encouraged; sharing solutions is not. A useful rule is that you may talk about a problem, but you should not leave the conversation with anything written down that the other person produced.

Generative AI

You may use generative AI tools to explain error messages or suggest study topics, but every query, diagram and paragraph you submit must be your own work, and you must declare any tool use on the coursework cover sheet. Pasting output from such a tool into a submission, even with edits, is treated in the same way as copying from any other unacknowledged source.

The reason for this rule is the purpose of the module. Its assessments test whether you can design and query a database yourself; work generated by a tool says nothing about that. If you are unsure whether a particular use is acceptable, ask the module leader before you submit, not after.

Attendance and lab sign-offs

Attendance at labs is recorded. Labs 3, 4 and 6 have sign-offs, in which a demonstrator checks your work and asks you to explain part of it. Sign-offs are not marked, but you need all three to be eligible for the resit opportunity if you fail the January examination. A sign-off can only be completed in your own timetabled session, up to the week stated in the lab sheet.

If you miss a lab for a reason that would justify an extension, tell the module administrator within a week, and a later session will be arranged for the sign-off.

The examination

The examination is a two-hour closed-book paper taken in person in the January assessment period. It has four questions, of which you answer three. Questions typically ask you to model a short scenario, normalise a given relation and justify the result, write and explain SQL queries, and reason about indexes or concurrent transactions in a described situation. Past papers from earlier years are on the module page, with outline answers released in the final teaching week.

You may bring a single A4 sheet of your own handwritten notes, written on both sides. Printed or photocopied notes are not allowed. Calculators are not needed and are not permitted.

Resits

If you fail the module overall, you are offered a resit opportunity in the summer assessment period for each failed component, subject to the progression rules. The resit for the coursework is a new brief of similar scope. Eligibility for the examination resit depends on the lab sign-offs described above; a student who has not completed them must repeat the module with attendance instead. Resit marks are capped at 40 unless the original attempt was affected by accepted extenuating circumstances.

Student representation

Each lab group elects a student representative in week 2. Representatives meet the module leader in weeks 5 and 10 to raise issues on behalf of their group, and the notes of those meetings are posted on the module page. Changes made in response to student feedback in previous years include recorded lectures, the extra worked examples in the normalisation lecture, and earlier publication of the coursework brief.

At the end of the module you will be asked to complete an anonymous survey. The responses are read by the module team and summarised for the programme committee, and they directly shape the next year's teaching.

Feedback

Marks and written feedback for Coursework 1 are released through the portal within three working weeks of the deadline. General feedback on common strengths and weaknesses is posted on the module page at the same time. Examination marks are released with the results for the whole assessment period; individual feedback on the examination is available on request.

Support

Questions about lectures, labs and coursework should go to the module discussion board, where the teaching team answers within two working days and every student can see the answer. Personal matters should go to the module leader by email. The Student Hub handles extensions, deferrals, timetabling and enrolment.

If you have a learning support plan, make sure the Student Hub has shared it with the module team in the first three weeks, so that adjustments for labs and the examination can be arranged in time.
