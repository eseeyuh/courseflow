SND210 Sound Design for Media - Coursework 1: Podcast mix portfolio

Quillmere Institute is a fictional institution. This material was written for the CourseFlow retrieval benchmark and describes no real course.

Overview

Coursework 1 asks you to take the raw multitrack recording of a twenty-minute interview podcast and turn it into a finished episode ready for distribution. The recording, made in the department studio, contains two presenters and one guest on separate microphones, a room tone track, an intro music cue and three short sound effects. It has the usual problems of a real session: one microphone picks up a noticeable hum, the guest moves closer to and further from the microphone, and there is a section where both presenters talk over each other.

You will edit, clean, balance and master the episode, and document the decisions you made and why. The coursework draws on the lectures on dynamics processing, equalisation, noise reduction and loudness, and on the studio sessions in weeks 2 to 5.

Coursework 1 is worth 50 percent of the module mark. The remaining 50 percent comes from the sound-for-picture project in the spring term.

Submission

Submit Coursework 1 through the module's coursework portal by 16:00 (UK time) on Friday 13 November 2026.

Coursework 1 is an individual assessment, although you may share studio bookings with other students as long as each of you produces your own mix.

The submission consists of the finished episode as a 48 kHz, 24-bit WAV file, a 128 kbps MP3 of the same episode, the session file from the digital audio workstation with all audio consolidated, and a production log of no more than 1,500 words.

What you must produce

  1. An edited episode with the cross-talk section made intelligible, false starts removed and pacing that suits the content.
  2. Clean dialogue: the hum removed or reduced without audible artefacts, and the guest's level made consistent.
  3. A balanced mix of dialogue, music and effects in which speech is always intelligible.
  4. A master that meets the delivery specification in the loudness lecture, with the measured integrated loudness and true peak reported in the production log.
  5. A production log explaining the main problems you found, what you did about each, and what you would do differently.

Marking scheme

ComponentWeightWhat earns high marks
Editing20%Natural pacing, inaudible edits, intelligible cross-talk.
Dialogue cleaning25%Problems fixed without introducing artefacts.
Mix balance25%Speech always clear; music and effects support rather than compete.
Mastering and delivery15%Meets the specification exactly; files named and formatted as required.
Production log15%Specific, honest, technically accurate reflection.

Delivery details

Name the WAV and MP3 files with your student number followed by _ep01, for example 21007731_ep01.wav. Both files must start with no more than half a second of silence before the first sound and end no more than two seconds after the last. The MP3 must carry an ID3 tag with the episode title, the programme name and your student number in the comment field.

The session file must open on a studio computer without missing media. Consolidate all audio into the session folder and test the folder on a second computer before you submit it. Plug-ins outside the studio's standard set may be used during mixing, but print their effect to audio before submission, because the markers' computers will not have them.

The production log is a PDF. Use headings for each problem you addressed, give timestamps so that a marker can find the passage, and state the measured integrated loudness, loudness range and maximum true peak of your delivered WAV and MP3. Screenshots of processing settings are welcome as an appendix and do not count towards the word limit.

Learning outcomes assessed

  • LO1: edit and clean spoken-word recordings to a professional standard.
  • LO2: balance dialogue, music and effects for intelligibility on varied playback systems.
  • LO3: master a programme to a stated delivery specification and verify it by measurement.
  • LO4: evaluate and justify production decisions in writing.

Advice

Listen to the whole recording before you change anything, and make notes with timestamps. Most problems are easier to fix once you know where all of them are. Check your mix on at least two playback systems, including headphones and a small speaker, because many listeners will hear the episode on a phone.
